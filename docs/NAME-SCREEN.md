# Telehealth brand name screen

**Run:** 2 September 2026 · **Outcome:** Millwright chosen. See
[`BRAND-AND-ENTITY-STRUCTURE.md`](./BRAND-AND-ENTITY-STRUCTURE.md) §6 for the decision
and the identity direction. This file is the evidence behind it.

> **This was a marketplace screen, not a trademark clearance.** The USPTO search system
> was not reachable, so every conflict below is evidence of a company *trading* under the
> name — a live site, a clinic, a product. A registered mark with no website would not
> appear here. "Clear" means *worth paying an attorney to search*, nothing stronger.
> Relevant class: **44** (medical services); a supplement line would also touch **5**.

## Clear — no operating company surfaced

**Millwright** *(chosen)* · **Whetstone** *(first runner-up)* · **Datum** *(second)* ·
Temper · Caliper · Ironbark · Flint · Bedrock · Crucible

Notes on the runners-up, in case Millwright fails its search:

- **Whetstone** — the stone an edge is kept on. Carries the same maintenance idea as
  Millwright and is far easier to spell; slightly less distinctive as a mark.
  `whetstoneclinic.com`, `whetstonemd.com`, `whetstonehormone.com` all open.
- **Datum** — the fixed point measurements are taken from. Safest to clear and the
  monogram is free (the ASME Y14.5 datum feature symbol is literally a boxed letter).
  But it is a *label*: it says nothing about what the clinic does for you, and it needs
  explaining just as much as Millwright does — so it buys spelling, not comprehension.
  `datumclinic.com`, `datummd.com`, `datumhormone.com` open.

## Ruled out on meaning, not conflict

- **Quarry** — the second meaning is "the thing being hunted." Not for patients.
- **Bellwether** — a wether is a castrated ram. Not for a testosterone clinic.
- **Meridian · Cornerstone · Benchmark** — saturated in healthcare. Low distinctiveness
  makes a weak mark and a hard SEO fight.

## The structural finding

`<name>health.com` was **taken or brokered for all 22 candidates**, without exception.
Any name chosen here needs a different construction. `<name>clinic.com` was free for nine
of the survivors and is the recommended pattern.

## Method

Domain state was read from live DNS: NS records at a broker (Afternic, Sedo, Dan.com,
NameFind, GoDaddy parking) were recorded as *for sale*, NS at a real operator as *taken*,
NXDOMAIN as *available*. Conflicts came from web search for each name in a healthcare,
hormone or TRT context. Both methods find operating businesses; neither finds the
register.
