## 0. How this document works

Under DSHEA, you — not your manufacturer — are legally responsible for the evidence behind every claim you make. The FTC standard is "competent and reliable scientific evidence." This binder is the file you produce if you receive an FDA warning letter, an FTC inquiry, or an NAD challenge from a competitor.

**Three rules for using it:**

1. **No claim ships that isn't in this binder.** Not on labels, not on the website, not in an ambassador's Instagram caption, not in a trainer's text to a client.
2. **Citations marked `[VERIFIED]`** were confirmed against the primary literature during preparation. Citations marked `[TO COMPILE]` are for ingredients with well-established literature where the specific papers still need to be pulled and PDF'd into the binder before launch. **Do not launch with `[TO COMPILE]` gaps.**
3. **Every branded ingredient supplier (Cognizin®, affron®, Creapure®, Magtein®, Dynamine®) must provide their clinical dossier in writing.** Request it as a condition of purchase. That dossier goes in this binder as an appendix and is a large part of what you're paying the premium for.

**Required on every label:**
> *These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.*

**Also required:** FDA structure/function claim notification within 30 days of first marketing a claim (21 CFR 101.93). Your co-man's regulatory team usually handles filing; confirm in writing who owns it.

## 1. Ingredient-Level Substantiation

### A-GRADE — claims may lead with these

---

**GLYCINE — 3,000 mg (DEN Nightcap) / 100 mg (DEN Pouch)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports sleep quality." · "Helps you fall asleep faster." · "Supports feeling refreshed on waking." · "Supports a natural drop in body temperature at bedtime."
- **Evidence:** Randomized crossover trials show 3 g before bedtime improves subjective sleep quality and reduces next-day fatigue, with polysomnographic correlates including shortened sleep-onset latency and faster entry to slow-wave sleep, without altering overall sleep architecture. Separate RCT evidence supports improved daytime performance following partial sleep restriction.
- **Citations:** `[VERIFIED]` Yamadera W et al. *Sleep and Biological Rhythms* 2007;5(2):126–131 (PSG crossover). · `[VERIFIED]` *Sleep Biol Rhythms* 2021;19(4):465–472, PMID 34107567 (double-blind RCT, insufficient sleep). · `[TO COMPILE]` Inagawa/Bannai partial sleep-restriction trials.
- **Dose justification:** 3 g matches the studied dose exactly. **This is our strongest claim in the PM line.**
- **Do NOT say:** "treats insomnia," "sedative," "knocks you out."

---

**L-THEANINE — 200 mg (Nightcap, Ritual) / 75–100 mg (pouches)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports focus and attention." · "Supports a calm, relaxed state without drowsiness." · "Helps smooth the edge off caffeine." · "Supports relaxation."
- **Evidence:** Extensive human RCT literature on L-theanine alone (relaxation, alpha-wave activity) and on the caffeine + L-theanine combination (attention, task switching, reduced jitters) at 50–200 mg.
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — well-established; pull Owen et al. *Nutr Neurosci* 2008; Haskell et al. 2008; Hidese et al. 2019 (theanine RCT); plus a recent systematic review. Supplier (Suntheanine® if used) will provide a dossier.
- **Note:** This is the safest, most defensible active in the entire portfolio. When in doubt about which ingredient to feature, feature this one.

---

**CAFFEINE — 75 mg/pouch · ~90–100 mg (Ritual coffee) · 200 mg (pre-workout)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports alertness and energy." · "Supports exercise performance." · "Helps reduce perceived effort during training."
- **Evidence:** The most extensively substantiated ergogenic aid in sports nutrition. Buccal/oral-mucosal delivery (caffeine gum literature, largely military) shows faster absorption than swallowed forms — this supports the pouch's onset claim specifically.
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — pull the ISSN position stand on caffeine and exercise performance (Guest et al. 2021, *JISSN*) as the anchor document; plus Kamimori et al. on caffeine gum pharmacokinetics for the buccal claim.
- **Required label warnings:** total caffeine per serving declared; "not for children under 18, pregnant or nursing women"; "do not combine with other stimulants"; max daily servings stated.
- **Do NOT say:** "no crash" as an absolute (say "formulated with L-theanine to smooth the caffeine curve"), "safer than energy drinks."

---

**MELATONIN — 0.5 mg**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports sleep onset." · "Low-dose, physiologic amount."
- **Evidence:** Large, well-established literature on melatonin and sleep-onset latency; low doses (0.3–1 mg) are effective for onset without the next-day residual effects seen at higher doses.
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — pull a recent meta-analysis on melatonin and sleep onset latency, plus a dose-response paper supporting the low-dose position (Zhdanova et al. is the classic).
- **Required warnings:** "Do not drive or operate machinery after use." · "Not for children." · "Consult a physician if pregnant, nursing, or taking sedatives or psychiatric medication."
- **Marketing angle that is fair game:** contrasting 0.5 mg against 5–10 mg products, framed as dosing philosophy, not as a safety claim about competitors.
- **Export note:** melatonin is restricted or prescription-only in several markets (UK, Australia, parts of the EU) — the melatonin-free Nightcap SKU is the export version.

---

**MAGNESIUM BISGLYCINATE — 200 mg elemental (Nightcap, Night Caps)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports sleep quality." · "Supports muscle relaxation." · "Supports normal nervous system function." · "Supports normal muscle function."
- **Evidence:** A 2025 randomized controlled trial (n=155) using 250 mg elemental magnesium bisglycinate reported improvement in insomnia severity scores. Broader magnesium literature supports muscle and nervous-system function claims; effects on sleep appear most pronounced in those with low baseline intake.
- **Citations:** `[VERIFIED — pull full text]` Schuster et al. 2025, magnesium bisglycinate RCT, n=155. · `[TO COMPILE]` Abbasi et al. 2012 (500 mg/day, elderly insomnia).
- **CRITICAL LABELING RULE:** declare **elemental magnesium**, not compound weight. A "500 mg magnesium glycinate" often contains only ~70 mg elemental. Our entire brand promise is honest dosing — this is the single most common deception in the category and we must be visibly on the right side of it.

---

**CREATINE MONOHYDRATE (Creapure®) — 5 g**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports strength and power output." · "Supports muscle mass with resistance training." · "Supports high-intensity exercise capacity." · "Supports cellular energy production."
- **Evidence:** Hundreds of human trials; among the best-substantiated sports supplements in existence.
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — anchor on the ISSN position stand on creatine (Kreider et al. 2017, *JISSN*). Creapure® (AlzChem) will supply purity documentation and a dossier.
- **Emerging claims — handle carefully:** cognitive and women's-health applications are an active research area but the evidence is younger. Phrase as "research is ongoing" in educational content; do not put cognitive claims on the label.

---

**SODIUM / POTASSIUM / MAGNESIUM (Tundra pouches, electrolyte sticks, H2 tablets)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports hydration." · "Supports electrolyte balance." · "Supports normal muscle function." · "Replaces electrolytes lost in sweat."
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — anchor on ACSM/NATA position statements on fluid replacement and exercise-associated muscle cramping.
- **Labeling rule:** elemental amounts only; be explicit that pouches deliver *trace* amounts, not full replacement. Never let a pouch imply it replaces a sports drink.
- **Do NOT say:** "prevents cramps" (say "supports normal muscle function"), "prevents heat illness," or anything medical.

**CITICOLINE (Cognizin®) — 250 mg (Ritual coffee, matcha)**

- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — request the full Cognizin® clinical dossier from Kyowa Hakko as a purchase condition; pull McGlade et al. attention trials independently.

**ALPHA-GPC — 100–150 mg (Charge pouch) / 300–600 mg (pre-workout)**

- **Approved claim language:** "Supports the mind-muscle connection." · "Acetylcholine precursor." · "Supports power output" *(pre-workout dose only)*.
- **Evidence:** Cognition and power-output data exist at 300–600 mg. **Below 300 mg the evidence thins substantially.**
- **Citations:** `[TO COMPILE]` — Ziegenfuss et al. 2008 (power output, 600 mg); Parker et al. 2015.
- **INTERNAL HONESTY NOTE:** the pouch dose (100–150 mg) is *below* studied doses. On pouch marketing, describe Alpha-GPC mechanistically ("acetylcholine precursor") and do not make power-output claims. Power claims belong to the pre-workout only, where the dose is clinical. Marketing must not blur these.
- **Handling:** hygroscopic. Use 50%-on-carrier form (e.g. AlphaSize 50P) in powders.

### C-GRADE — present for consumer expectation only. NEVER headline. NEVER claim outcomes.

**LION'S MANE (Hericium erinaceus) — 1,000 mg fruiting body**

- **Permitted language:** "Contains hericenones and erinacines, compounds studied for their role in nerve growth factor signaling." · "Traditionally used to support cognitive wellness." · Ingredient-quality claims: "fruiting body only," "beta-glucans tested and declared."
- **Honest evidence position:** A 2025 systematic review of five RCTs found a weighted mean improvement of approximately 1.17 MMSE points versus placebo — a real but modest effect concentrated in people with mild cognitive impairment. Trials in healthy younger adults have not shown benefit; a single-dose crossover study (n=18) found no effect on cognition or mood, and at least one trial reported *worse* delayed word recall. Where effects appear, they build over weeks (2–16) and decline after cessation.
- **Citations:** `[VERIFIED]` Mori et al. 2009 (MCI, 3 g/day, 16 wk). · `[VERIFIED]` Surendran et al. *Front Nutr* 2025;12:1405796 (acute, null). · `[VERIFIED]` 2025 *Frontiers in Nutrition* systematic review, 5 RCTs, WMD 1.17 MMSE. · `[VERIFIED]` Alzheimer's Drug Discovery Foundation Cognitive Vitality review — mixed results.
- **DO NOT SAY:** "improves memory," "boosts cognition," "nootropic that works," "brain fog cure," or anything implying Alzheimer's/dementia benefit. **This is the highest-risk ingredient in the portfolio for claim overreach because the category markets it irresponsibly. Marketing team: read this entry twice.**

Keep this list in the binder. It demonstrates a good-faith safety process, which matters enormously if you're ever challenged.

| **GABA** | Poor blood-brain-barrier penetration; effects largely peripheral. Excluded for efficacy, not safety. |
| **Valerian** | Mixed efficacy; organoleptically incompatible with a premium flavor system. |
| **High-dose melatonin (3–10 mg)** | Not superior for sleep onset; associated with next-day residual effects. |
| **BCAAs (as a standalone product)** | Redundant when protein intake is adequate; weak independent evidence. |

## 4. Universal Prohibited Language

Never, in any channel, by anyone, including ambassadors:

- Any disease name: insomnia, ADHD/ADD, anxiety, depression, dementia, Alzheimer's, arthritis, diabetes, long COVID.
- Any of: *treats, cures, prevents, heals, reverses, manages, diagnoses, remedy, therapy, medicine, drug-free alternative to [drug name].*
- Any comparison to a pharmaceutical: "like Adderall," "natural Ambien," "better than melatonin pills."
- Any implication of quitting nicotine (see below).
- Unqualified superlatives: "the most effective," "clinically proven" *(unless the specific finished product was clinically tested — ours won't be at launch).*
- Before/after physique photos with implied causation from a supplement.

## 5. Two Traps Specific to This Business

**A. The nicotine-cessation trap.** Marketing STEADY as a way to quit nicotine pouches makes it an unapproved smoking-cessation *drug*. This is a well-trodden FDA enforcement area. What you may safely do: describe the product truthfully ("nicotine-free," "tobacco-free," "satisfies the pouch habit without nicotine") and let customers draw their own conclusion. Do not use the words "quit," "cessation," "wean," "taper," or "replacement therapy." Do not repost customer testimonials that say "helped me quit" — repeating a customer's drug claim makes it your claim.

**B. The TherapyLog intended-use trap.** If supplement marketing appears inside or adjacent to a mental-health app, that context can be used as evidence of intended use. A "focus" supplement promoted to an audience of people managing ADHD, inside an app about mental health, is materially riskier than the same product on a general fitness site. Mitigations: keep supplement promotion in the app strictly brand-level; never segment or target supplement offers using health, mood, symptom, or diagnosis data from the app; never place supplement offers adjacent to symptom-tracking or diagnosis-related screens; have counsel review any in-app placement before it ships. And keep the peptide business entirely separate, as previously discussed.

## 6. Ambassador & Influencer Claim Control

Every ambassador agreement must include:

1. **Approved claims appendix** — a one-page list of exactly what they may say, drawn from §3. Simple rule for them: *"Say how it makes you feel. Never say what it does to a condition."*
2. **FTC disclosure requirement** — clear and conspicuous #ad or #ArctosPartner in every post, not buried in a hashtag block.
3. **No medical claims clause** with a right of immediate termination.
4. **No testimonials about conditions** — including their own. "This fixed my ADHD focus" from a trainer is a claim attributable to you.
5. **Annual re-acknowledgment** and a monthly content spot-check. Log the checks — documented monitoring is a mitigating factor in enforcement.
