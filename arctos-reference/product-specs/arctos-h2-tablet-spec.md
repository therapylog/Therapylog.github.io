# ARCTOS H2 — Molecular Hydrogen Tablet Spec
*Evidence review · chemistry constraints · signature product design*

---

## 1. What the evidence actually supports

Molecular hydrogen is one of the more interesting things in this category: a real mechanism, a growing body of human trials, and — unusually for a trendy ingredient — **effects that cluster in a specific, honest place.** That place is endurance, perceived effort, heart-rate response, and recovery. Not strength. Not power.

**What the human trials show:**

- **Endurance and perceived exertion.** In 22 amateur middle-distance runners, 500 mL of hydrogen-rich water 30 minutes pre-exercise improved time to exhaustion, rate of perceived exertion, and peak heart rate versus placebo — **but showed no effect on squat jump, countermovement jump, or five-jump test.** That split is the single most useful fact in the whole literature: H2 helps how hard it feels and how long you last, not how hard you hit.
- **Heart rate at submaximal intensity.** A double-blind placebo-controlled crossover pilot (n=19) using hydrogen-producing tablets delivering 5 mg H2 concluded they may benefit submaximal aerobic performance by lowering exercising heart rate, while noting that optimal dosing and chronic-use effects still need work.
- **Recovery from repeated same-day sessions.** A randomized double-blind crossover trial in elite fin swimmers found hydrogen-rich water promoted muscle recovery after two strenuous sessions in one day, measuring countermovement jump, creatine kinase, and soreness through 24 hours.
- **Pooled analysis.** A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Frontiers in Nutrition* (2023) examined molecular hydrogen for fatigue and aerobic capacity in healthy adults — the appropriate anchor citation for a fatigue/endurance claim.
- **Safety at scale.** A phase 3, triple-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (n=675) used hydrogen-producing tablets (1 tablet in 250 mL, twice daily for 21 days). Whatever you think of the indication, it's a large, well-controlled human safety dataset for exactly the delivery format you'd be selling. That's genuinely useful for your file.

**Honest grade: B for endurance, perceived exertion, and recovery. C for everything else.** Trials are mostly small, several are pilots, and a chunk of the field is industry-adjacent. That's fine — it just means your marketing lives in "supports recovery and endurance," never in "reduces inflammation and oxidative stress" as an outcome claim.

**⚠️ The claim minefield:** hydrogen water is marketed appallingly — cancer, autism, long COVID, anti-aging. The category's reputation is bad *because* of that marketing. Your entire opportunity is being the credible one. Zero disease claims, ever, and never cite the COVID trial in consumer marketing even though it's in your evidence file.

## 2. Chemistry constraints — read before designing the tablet

This is where most brands go wrong, and where your manufacturer conversation needs to be precise.

**How the tablet works:** elemental (metallic) magnesium + a food acid (usually malic or tartaric) + binders. Dropped in water, the magnesium reacts to release H2 gas and leaves magnesium hydroxide/magnesium salts behind. Notably, in the phase 3 trial the placebo was identical *except* it used magnesium carbonate instead of metallic magnesium — which tells you the metallic magnesium is the entire active mechanism.

**Four consequences you must design around:**

1. **Your H2 tablet is already a magnesium supplement.** A typical tablet leaves roughly 40–80 mg of elemental magnesium in the glass. Confirm the exact figure with your manufacturer and **declare it on the panel.** This is an asset — it's a second honest claim for free — but it also means you must not stack it carelessly with a full magnesium dose elsewhere in the same protocol.

2. **You cannot add much to the tablet itself.** The tablet is a moisture-triggered reactive system. Anything you add must survive compression, a low-moisture environment, and an acid reaction — and it all ends up in the glass affecting taste and clarity. Heat- and oxidation-sensitive actives, most botanicals, and anything with a strong flavor are poor candidates. **Do not let a manufacturer talk you into a "loaded" hydrogen tablet.** It will either degrade or taste bad.

3. **H2 escapes fast.** Hydrogen is the smallest molecule there is; it diffuses out of an open glass within minutes. The product only works if the user drops it in a **sealed** bottle, waits for full dissolution (usually 1–3 minutes), and drinks it promptly. If your instructions are unclear, your product doesn't work and your reviews will say so. This is a consumer-education problem more than a formulation problem.

4. **The blister pack is functional, not just branding.** These tablets react with ambient humidity. Individual sealed cavities are the correct packaging for a real reason — which gives you a great line: *"Each tablet is sealed individually because moisture is the enemy. Open it, drop it, cap it, drink it."*

## 3. The signature product: solve the chemistry with two tablets

Here's the design that makes this a flagship rather than a commodity.

You can't load the H2 tablet — but you *can* put **two tablets in one blister cavity pair.**

> ### ARCTOS H2 · THE TWO-TABLET PROTOCOL
> **Tablet 1 — the H2 core** (gold): metallic magnesium + malic acid. Unflavored. Its only job is producing hydrogen, done properly, at a verified 5+ mg dose.
> **Tablet 2 — the TUNDRA companion** (forest green): full electrolyte and performance load, effervescent, flavored.
>
> Drop both. Cap the bottle. 90 seconds. Drink.

This is genuinely better product design, not just marketing: it lets each tablet be optimized for its own chemistry, fixes the flat mineral aftertaste H2 tablets leave, and creates a ritual that photographs beautifully — **two tablets, gold and green, dropping into a bottle.** That's your hero video. It also physically embodies the brand's two-color identity.

### Companion tablet formula (Tablet 2)

| Ingredient | Dose | Grade | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium (as bicarbonate + chloride) | 300–500 mg | A | Hydration + sweat replacement. Bicarbonate additionally contributes to buffering, though a full ergogenic bicarb load (0.2–0.3 g/kg ≈ 20 g) is far beyond tablet scale — **claim hydration, not buffering.** |
| Potassium (citrate) | 200 mg elemental | A | Standard electrolyte; check per-serving regulatory limits with your co-man. |

| **L-Citrulline** | 1,000 mg | B at this dose | Below the 6–8 g pump dose, so **claim blood-flow support, not "pump."** Honest partial dosing, disclosed. Optional — cut if it bulks the tablet too much. |

| Vitamin C | ❌ omit | — | **Deliberately excluded.** High-dose antioxidant vitamins can blunt training adaptations, and adding one undercuts the entire premise that H2 is a *selective* antioxidant. Say so in your education content — it's a sophisticated argument your science-literate audience will respect. |
| Flavor/acid system | — | — | Citric/malic acid, monk fruit, natural flavor. Zero sugar. |

**Flavor:** launch with two — **Arctic Citrus** (bright, clean, universal) and **Salted Watermelon** (proven best-seller shape in electrolytes). Add an **Unflavored Purist** later to match the Tundra pouch philosophy.

### Packaging

Custom blister strips are ideal here. Recommended: **10-dose strips** (10 gold + 10 green cavities, paired), forest-green foil backing with gold-foil bear printed on each cavity — so the branding is visible on every single dose, which is the cheapest brand impression you'll ever buy. Retail carton in the master carton system you already spec'd, QR-to-COA in the uncoated knockout zone.

Two SKUs: **10-dose travel strip** (trial/sampling — perfect trainer giveaway) and **30-dose carton** (subscription unit).

### Accessory play

An **Arctos H2 bottle** — insulated, sealed, gold bear on matte forest green — is a natural high-margin accessory and solves the "must use a sealed container" education problem in one move. Bundle it with the 30-dose carton as a starter kit. Accessories also give trainers something to *win* in a challenge/incentive program rather than just discount codes.

## 4. Positioning: where H2 sits in the lineup

H2's honest strength — endurance, perceived effort, recovery — is exactly the gap your current lineup has. CHARGE is stimulant energy. TUNDRA is minerals. Creatine is strength and power. **Nothing yet owns "the session felt easier and I recovered faster."**

| Product | Owns |
|---|---|
| CHARGE | Drive and alertness |
| **ARCTOS H2** | **Endurance, perceived effort, recovery** |
| TUNDRA | Hydration |
| PRIME CREATINE | Strength and power |
| DEN | Sleep and repair |

**Approved claim language:** *Supports endurance and exercise capacity · Supports recovery after training · Supports reduced perceived exertion · Selective antioxidant support · 5+ mg molecular hydrogen per tablet, verified.*

**Never say:** reduces inflammation *(disease-adjacent)* · anti-aging · detox · alkaline water benefits · anything about a disease · anything referencing the COVID literature.

**The differentiator to hammer:** most hydrogen products can't tell you how much H2 they actually deliver, and many deliver almost none. **Publish the measured H2 concentration per tablet on the COA behind your QR code.** In a category built on unverifiable gas, being the brand that measures and publishes is the whole game — and it's the same "verify yours" promise already running through your carton spec, creatine tub, and pouch cans.

## 5. Questions to put to your manufacturer in writing

1. Measured H2 concentration delivered per tablet, in mg and ppm, in a sealed 500 mL container — **with third-party verification, not internal data.**
2. Full dissolution time, and H2 retention curve over 5/10/30 minutes in a sealed vs. open vessel.
3. Elemental magnesium residual per tablet (needed for the Supplement Facts panel).
4. Shelf life and stability data — specifically, moisture sensitivity and blister barrier specification (foil/foil, not foil/PVC, for a moisture-reactive tablet).
5. Can they blister-pack **two different tablets** in paired cavities on one strip? (This is the make-or-break question for the signature concept. If they can't, ask whether a second supplier can co-pack.)
6. Facility: FDA-registered, cGMP under 21 CFR 111 (dietary supplements)?
7. Per-lot CoA: identity, heavy metals, micro, **plus measured H2 output.**
8. MOQ, lead time, and pricing at 10k / 25k / 50k dose tiers.
9. Do they hold any exclusivity or formula IP that limits your ability to move manufacturers later? (Ask early — this is where small brands get trapped.)
