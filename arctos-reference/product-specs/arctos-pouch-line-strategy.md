## 1. Formulation Review of the Gemini Draft

The overall concept is sound: buccal/sublingual delivery is real (it's why nicotine pouches work), the 600 mg fill weight with 40–60% active loading matches how contract pouch manufacturers actually build products, and the fasting/keto/carnivore positioning is a genuine wedge into an audience mainstream brands ignore. But there are several problems that need to be fixed before this goes to a manufacturer.

### 🚨 Critical: Remove Vinpocetine Entirely

This is the one non-negotiable change. The FDA has taken the formal position that **vinpocetine does not meet the definition of a dietary ingredient** and is excluded from the definition of a dietary supplement (it was authorized for investigation as a new drug before supplement marketing). On top of the regulatory status, FDA issued a 2019 safety warning that vinpocetine may cause miscarriage or harm fetal development, advising women of childbearing age not to take it. The Department of Defense's Operation Supplement Safety flags it as prohibited in supplements — meaning any military customers (a big gym demographic near bases) would be told to avoid your product.

For a premium brand built on "hyper-clean" trust, launching with an ingredient on FDA's advisory list is a brand-killer and an easy target for an FDA warning letter. Drop it.

**Replacement candidates for the "smooth cerebral energy" slot:**

| Ingredient | Why it works in a pouch | Suggested dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Methylliberine (Dynamine®)** | Caffeine-adjacent alkaloid, fast onset (~15–30 min), shorter half-life so no sleep disruption, no reported tolerance buildup. Branded/patented = premium story + supplier CoAs. | 25–50 mg |
| **Theacrine (TeaCrine®)** | Slower, smoother energy with minimal tolerance in studies; pairs with caffeine synergistically. | 25–50 mg |
| **Saffron extract (affron®)** | Mood/focus angle, clinically studied, very small dose fits pouch constraints. | 14–28 mg |

Dynamine is the best fit for the Focus line — it keeps the "fast, clean, sharp" narrative and has actual buccal-plausible pharmacokinetics.

### ⚠️ Honest-Dosing Problems (the "fairy dusting" risk)

The premium-clean audience you're targeting *reads labels and knows clinical doses*. Two ingredients in the draft are dosed far below anything with evidence:

- **Alpha-GPC at 50 mg (Focus) / 20 mg (Stim-Free).** Clinical doses for cognition/power output are 300–600 mg. The buccal-absorption argument ("you need less") is plausible but has essentially zero published data for Alpha-GPC specifically. At 20 mg you're at ~3–6% of a clinical dose. Options: (a) raise Focus-line Alpha-GPC to 150 mg and make it the headline active, (b) market honestly as "stacking accumulates your dose across pouches," or (c) swap to **huperzine A (50–100 mcg)** which IS effective at microgram doses and fits pouch weight limits perfectly — though it should be cycled and shouldn't be in an "all-day habitual" product. Recommendation: keep Alpha-GPC at 100–150 mg in Focus only, remove from Stim-Free.
- **L-Tyrosine at 50 mg / NALT at 20 mg.** Clinical tyrosine doses are 500–2,000 mg. Worse, the NALT framing in the Gemini draft is backwards: NALT is marketed as superior but human data shows poor conversion to free tyrosine (most excreted unchanged). If you keep a tyrosine story, use free-form L-tyrosine and be honest that it's a supporting dose — or cut it and put the milligrams into ingredients that work at pouch-scale doses.

**The core design principle:** a 600 mg pouch gives you ~240–360 mg of active budget. Build the formula around ingredients that are *actually effective at ≤100 mg* rather than sprinkling ineffective doses of ingredients that need grams. Caffeine, theanine, Dynamine, huperzine, B12, melatonin, saffron, and minerals all pass this test. Tyrosine and Alpha-GPC mostly don't.

### ✅ What's Right and Should Stay

- **Caffeine 75 mg + L-theanine:** The one truly evidence-backed buccal stack. Caffeine absorbs well through oral mucosa (faster onset than swallowed). Suggest bumping theanine to 75–100 mg — the studied ratio is 1:1 to 2:1 theanine:caffeine, and theanine is cheap, safe, and tastes fine.
- **Methylcobalamin B12 sublingual:** Legitimately well-established sublingual delivery. Keep at 500 mcg – 1 mg.
- **Two-pouch stacking to 150 mg caffeine:** Smart. It also keeps single-pouch caffeine at a level that's safe for the "habitual all-day user" and under scrutiny thresholds.
- **Monk fruit + cellulose base, zero-carb:** Correct for the audience. Resist any co-manufacturer pushing maltitol/xylitol blends — "monk fruit only" is a cleaner claim and a marketing line.

### Mineral Line Adjustments

The concept (savory micro-hydration, saliva stimulation, keto/carnivore) is genuinely underserved. Fixes:

1. **Magnesium glycinate 25 mg is only ~3.5 mg elemental magnesium** — a rounding error. Either state elemental amounts honestly on the label (required anyway) and position as "trace mineral maintenance," or switch to **magnesium malate or citrate** for slightly better density. Do not imply meaningful magnesium repletion from a pouch.
2. **Potassium citrate 50 mg ≈ 19 mg elemental potassium.** Same honesty rule. (FDA also historically limits potassium per unit dose in some formats — your co-man will confirm limits for pouches.)
3. **Positioning fix:** market this line as *"micro-dosed electrolytes + saliva flow + oral habit replacement"* — not as hydration replacement. Then upsell your future electrolyte *powder* as the full-dose companion ("Pouch for the field, powder for the bottle"). That's a clean two-product ladder instead of one product overpromising.
4. The **"Pure Purist" unflavored raw salt SKU is brilliant** — near-zero flavor R&D cost, perfect carnivore credibility signal, great PR hook. Keep it.

### Flavor System Warnings

- **Turmeric will stain.** Curcumin dyes teeth, gums, and the white pouch paper bright yellow. For a premium brand, a product that yellows customers' teeth is fatal. Either use a *flavor-only* ginger extract with no curcuminoids, or drop turmeric from pouches entirely and save it for powders.
- **Wintergreen = methyl salicylate.** Fine at flavor levels, but specify food-grade flavor concentrations with your co-man and keep it out of any product a user might go through 15+ pouches/day with (salicylate load). Peppermint is the safer default.
- Coffee/espresso flavor in a white pouch also risks staining — ask the manufacturer for a stain test on the pouch paper.

### Revised Line Architecture (post-fixes)

| Line | Actives per pouch | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus (Ultra-Strength)** | Caffeine 75 mg · L-theanine 75 mg · Dynamine 40 mg · Alpha-GPC 100–150 mg · B12 250 mcg | Pre-workout / morning drive; stack ×2 for training |
| **Stim-Free All-Day** | L-theanine 50 mg · Saffron 14 mg (or huperzine 50 mcg on a "cycle" SKU) · B12 500 mcg | Habitual use, oral fixation, evening-safe |
| **Mineral (Zero-Carb)** | Sea salt → 80 mg sodium · K-citrate 50 mg · Mg 25 mg | Keto/carnivore, training, "purist" flagship |
| **PM Recovery** *(new — see §4)* | Melatonin 0.5–1 mg · L-theanine 100 mg · Apigenin 25 mg (or glycine) | Sleep/wind-down; completes the 24-hour cycle |

## 2. Manufacturer Strategy

The functional-pouch contract manufacturing market has matured fast — you do not need to build this in-house.

**What the landscape looks like:** US and Nordic/EU contract manufacturers now run nicotine-free functional pouches (caffeine, nootropic, energy) as a standard offering. Typical terms in this niche: custom multi-active runs start around **5,000–25,000 cans MOQ**, lead times **6–14 weeks**, with some US manufacturers offering white-label starts at ~25,000 cans (flexible at ~5,000 per flavor) and low-MOQ shops advertising from ~2,500 cans per formula. FDA-registered, GMP-certified US facilities exist specifically for this format (e.g., Daily Manufacturing Solutions, PouchWorx, and several others aggregated on sourcing platforms like Wonnda).

**Recommended sequence:**

1. **Shortlist 4–6 manufacturers:** filter hard for (a) FDA-registered facility, (b) cGMP for *dietary supplements* (21 CFR 111) — not just tobacco-alternative GMP, (c) experience blending multi-active stacks, (d) willingness to run your custom formula rather than relabeling stock recipes.
2. **Send a blind spec sheet first** (actives, doses, base, sweetener, target moisture/pH, flavor directions) — without brand name — and ask for: per-1,000-can pricing at 3 volume tiers, MOQ, lead time, stability data process, and third-party CoA policy.
3. **Demand blend-uniformity verification.** The defining failure mode in multi-active pouches is uneven distribution of the low-dose actives (your B12, huperzine, Dynamine). Ask specifically how they verify uniformity for the *lowest*-inclusion ingredient across a batch. A manufacturer without a good answer is disqualified.
4. **Third-party testing = your COA story.** You already built COA/QR verification into the Arctos packaging spec. Apply the same system here: every lot third-party tested (identity, potency, heavy metals, micro), COA behind the QR knockout label. This is a *massive* differentiator in the pouch category where fairy-dusting is rampant — "Scan for COA" on a supplement pouch is nearly unique.
5. **Start with 2 SKUs, not 6+ flavors.** One Focus flavor (peppermint) + Pure Purist mineral. Lowest MOQ exposure, tests both the stim and carnivore audiences, and Purist requires no flavor development.
6. **Certifications to pursue in order:** third-party lot testing at launch → Informed Sport / NSF Certified for Sport once revenue supports it (opens college athletes, military, tested athletes — huge for trainer credibility).

## 3. Ingredient Research Summary (for your claims file)

Keep a substantiation binder — under DSHEA you're responsible for evidence behind structure/function claims. The safe claim architecture per ingredient:

- **Caffeine:** alertness, endurance performance, reduced perceived exertion — the single most-substantiated ergogenic aid; buccal/gum delivery shown to speed absorption vs. swallowing (military caffeine-gum literature).
- **L-theanine (+caffeine):** attention and focus with reduced jitters — multiple RCTs on the combination at 50–100 mg doses. Your best-supported claim set.
- **Alpha-GPC:** acetylcholine precursor; power-output and cognition data exists at 300–600 mg (dose honestly or frame as stack-accumulation).
- **Dynamine/TeaCrine:** energy and mood without tolerance; supplier-funded human studies exist — licensing the branded ingredient gets you their substantiation dossier (this is a real benefit of branded ingredients for a small company).
- **Huperzine A:** acetylcholinesterase inhibition at 50–200 *mcg*; effective at pouch-compatible doses; advise cycling.
- **Methylcobalamin:** normal energy-yielding metabolism claims; sublingual delivery well-established.
- **Sodium/potassium/magnesium:** electrolyte balance, muscle function, hydration support — standard, safe claims; state elemental amounts.
- **Melatonin (PM line):** sleep onset; sublingual formats well-precedented; keep at 0.5–1 mg ("low-dose, physiologic" is the premium/biohacker-respected position vs. 10 mg gas-station gummies).
- **Apigenin:** emerging (Huberman-audience) sleep ingredient; thinner evidence — pair with theanine so the claim rests on the stronger ingredient.

Never claim: treats ADHD, cures anything, replaces medication. "Supports focus" ✅ / "manages ADHD" ❌ — the latter makes it an unapproved drug. This matters double for you because of the TherapyLog adjacency (see §7).

## 4. New Line Recommendation: "PM Recovery" — the underserved niche

The pouch market is saturated with energy and nicotine-replacement. It has almost **nothing for sleep and wind-down**, despite (a) sublingual melatonin being one of the oldest proven sublingual delivery formats, and (b) the exact same oral-fixation behavior existing at night (the "scrolling in bed with a ZYN in" user). 

A **PM pouch — melatonin 0.5–1 mg + L-theanine 100 mg + apigenin 25 mg, chamomile-lavender or "honey mint" flavor** — completes a 24-hour product ecosystem no competitor has:

> **AM:** Focus pouch → **All day:** Stim-Free → **Training:** Minerals → **Night:** PM Recovery

That full-cycle story is also the perfect trainer/ambassador pitch: "your clients' entire day, one tin at a time." Secondary niche worth testing later: a **"Quit" positioning** (not a new SKU — a marketing angle) explicitly targeting nicotine-pouch users who want off nicotine; the Stim-Free line is functionally built for this already.

One product NOT to build as a pouch: creatine. It needs 3–5 g/day — physically impossible in a 600 mg pouch, and any "creatine pouch" would be pure fairy dust. Creatine belongs in the powder line (§5), where it's your best product.

## 5. Athlete Product Expansion — Fastest ROI First

Ranked by (margin × demand × low complexity × brand fit), here's the reinvestment ladder:

**Tier 1 — launch within 6 months of pouches:**

1. **Creatine Monohydrate (Creapure®, unflavored + one flavor).** The best ROI product in all of sports nutrition right now: raw material is cheap, demand is exploding beyond bodybuilders (women's health, longevity, cognitive angles), it's consumable monthly (subscription), and "German Creapure + third-party COA via QR" fits the Arctos premium-verification identity perfectly. Black matte tub, gold foil bear, forest green label — it will look like nothing else on the creatine shelf.
2. **Electrolyte powder sticks (LMNT-style: ~1,000 mg sodium / 200 mg potassium / 60 mg magnesium, zero sugar).** Direct extension of the Mineral pouch line — same audience, same claims, full-dose companion product. Stick packs have premium unit economics, low MOQ co-manufacturing is abundant, and the keto/carnivore market has proven willingness to pay $1.50+/serving.

**Tier 2 — months 6–12:**

3. **Pre-workout powder.** High AOV and demand but a brutally crowded category; differentiate on the same honesty platform: full clinical doses, full label transparency, no proprietary blends, COA per lot. Core formula: caffeine 200 mg, L-citrulline 6–8 g, beta-alanine 3.2 g, betaine 2.5 g, taurine 1–2 g, theanine 100 mg. Your pouch customers are the warm audience.

**Tier 3 — month 12+ (capital intensive, do last):**

4. **Protein.** Highest revenue potential, worst startup economics: expensive MOQs, heavy shipping, thin margins, flavor R&D. When you get there, the brand-correct move is a **grass-fed whey isolate** AND a **beef protein isolate + colostrum** SKU for the carnivore audience — the bear logo was born for a beef isolate ("Arctos Primal" writes itself). "Nutritionally complete" positioning = add digestive enzymes, full EAA disclosure, and pair with a micronutrient story rather than chasing "meal replacement" claims.

Everything ships in the established identity: PMS 5535 forest green, gold foil shield, matte black + soft-touch, QR-to-COA on every unit. In supplements, where everything is neon and screaming, a quiet luxury system with verifiable testing is the differentiation.

## 6. Gym & Trainer Ambassador Framework

Your instinct (10% customer discount / 15% commission) is close to right and sits at the generous end of the market — fitness affiliate programs typically run 3–20%, with premium supplement brands like Onnit at 12–15% and most big brands at 5–10%. Because pouches carry very high gross margins (~70–80% at premium pricing), you can afford to be the best-paying program a trainer has ever seen — which is itself a recruiting pitch.

**Recommended two-track structure:**

**Track A — Trainer/Ambassador codes (D2C):**

| Tier | Requirement | Customer gets | Trainer earns | Extras |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze | Signed up | 10% off | 15% of net sales | Free starter kit (4 tins) |
| Silver | $500/mo in code sales, 3 consecutive mo. | 10% off | 20% | Free monthly product box, early access |
| Gold | $1,500/mo | 15% off | 20% + $100 monthly bonus | Co-branded content, event sponsorship, input on flavors |

Key mechanics: pay on *net* sales (after discount), 30-day cookie plus permanent code attribution, monthly payout at $50 minimum, and — most important — **commission applies to repeat purchases by their referred customers for 12 months.** Recurring commission on consumables is what makes trainers actually push it, since one converted client = ongoing income.

**Track B — Gym wholesale (counter sales):**

- Wholesale at 50% off MSRP (standard 2× keystone), MOQ one case (e.g., 15 tins), free branded counter display.
- Offer consignment for the first case to remove all risk from the gym owner — you only get paid on sell-through. This is the single most effective door-opener with independent gyms.
- Gyms with wholesale accounts can ALSO have a staff ambassador code for their online push — the two tracks compound.

**The pitch sequence (what actually works walking into a gym):**

1. **Product first, pitch second.** Hand the owner/trainers free tins with zero ask: "Try these for two weeks, I'll come back." Pouches are novel — the product does the selling.
2. **Return visit pitch, 60 seconds:** "Everything's third-party tested — scan the tin, the COA comes up. Zero carbs, fast-safe, so it fits every client you have on keto, carnivore, or fasted training. Your clients ask you what to take; this gives you an answer you earn 15–20% on, every month, including their reorders."
3. **The comparison close:** "Onnit pays 15% once. We pay 15–20% forever on your people, and the tin costs your client less than their pre-workout."
4. **Leave-behinds:** one-page laminated sheet (brand story + commission table + QR to sign up), 10 sample pouches in single-serve sachets (ask your co-man for sample sachets — cheap and standard), counter display offer.
5. **Target order:** independent/boutique gyms and CrossFit boxes first (owner is the decision-maker, on the floor). Franchise gyms (Anytime, Crunch) have corporate vendor rules — skip until you have traction. Jiu-jitsu/MMA gyms are a sleeper market: pouch culture is already massive there via nicotine, and a nicotine-free performance pouch is an easy conversion.

**Guardrails:** written ambassador agreement including FTC disclosure requirements (they must disclose the affiliate relationship in posts) and a claims clause — ambassadors may not make medical/ADHD/disease claims about the products. One trainer posting "cured my ADHD" creates FDA exposure for *you*.

## 7. Business & Legal Structure: TherapyLog, Peptides, Supplements

Short version: **three rails, and the peptide rail must be fully isolated.**

**Entity architecture:**

```
        [Holding Co LLC]  (optional but recommended)
         /       |        \
  TherapyLog   Arctos      [Separate name] Research
  (software)   Nutrition    Supply LLC (RUO peptides)
               LLC (pouches,
               powders, protein)
```

1. **Supplements + TherapyLog can coexist and cross-promote — carefully.** A wellness app marketing "clean supplements" is a normal, legal pairing (structure/function claims only, standard DSHEA disclaimers). One real caution: if TherapyLog touches mental-health/therapy data, be careful about *targeting supplement ads based on user health data* — that raises FTC/health-privacy issues (Health Breach Notification Rule territory) and, frankly, trust issues with your users. Cross-promote at the brand level ("from the makers of"), not via health-data-driven targeting. Also keep the Focus line's marketing inside structure/function lines ("supports focus and attention") especially in any ADHD-adjacent app context — the app context can be used as evidence of *intended use*, and "intended to manage ADHD" = unapproved drug.

2. **The RUO peptide business must be a separate entity, separate brand name, separate website, separate bank account, no shared marketing, no shared customer lists.** This isn't just liability hygiene — it's the substance of the legal position. RUO products are lawful only when genuinely sold for laboratory research; regulators and courts look at the *totality of marketing context* to determine intended use. If the same brand (or visibly linked brands) sells human supplements, runs a therapy/wellness app, and sells "research" peptides with COA verification and consumer-friendly packaging, the whole picture screams intended human consumption — and it would put the *supplement* business and the app at risk in an enforcement action, not just the peptide entity. Concretely:
   - Different LLC, different trade name, no "Arctos" family resemblance, no bear.
   - No links between the websites, no cross-coupons, no shared Instagram.
   - B2B posture: sell to labs/institutions where possible, keep RUO disclaimers, no dosing guidance, no "wellness" language anywhere.
   - Do **not** market peptides "alongside or in the TherapyLog environment" — that specific idea is the one to kill. An app about human wellness recommending "research chemicals" is the cleanest intended-use evidence an enforcement attorney could ask for.
   - Watch the current regulatory window: FDA's compounding-category review of peptides is active in 2026, and the legitimate human-use pathway that's opening runs through licensed 503A/503B compounding pharmacies with prescriptions — not consumer brands. If you want a *legal* human-peptide play long-term, the move is partnering with/serving licensed compounders or a telehealth+compounding model, which is a different business with real licensing requirements. Get an FDA regulatory attorney for one focused session before the peptide entity sells anything.

3. **The holding company** gives you consolidated ownership, lets profits from fast-ROI products (pouches, creatine) fund the slower plays, and keeps liability firewalled. Standard setup; any competent business attorney can do it. Keep separate books, separate insurance (product liability policy for the supplement co is non-negotiable — ~$1–3k/yr at startup scale), and don't commingle funds.
